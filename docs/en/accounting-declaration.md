# Appendix B — An Important Declaration on the Accounting

P-Lens does not make light of the financial burden that comes with play.

It records the revenue and the expenses of play.

It records the acquisition and the using up of tokens.

It records the balls and medals saved at each parlor.

Where needed, it records the terms of exchange, differences in valuation and adjustments of every kind.

It records the acquisition of special prizes and of odd prizes too.

But bookkeeping has a boundary.

On events it does not record, the book is silent.

That silence is not a denial of reality.

It is merely the boundary of an accounting model.

That is all.
