# P-Lens Accounting Standards

— Accounting policy for recording the economic events of play and for prize exchange —

Pレンズ遊技会計基準 (PLAS)

- Edition: 1.0
- Enacted: 13 October 2026
- Scope: P-Lens and those who use its accounting model
- Base currencies: Japanese yen, and the balls and medals particular to each parlor
- Basic principles: debits equal credits, honesty of the record, silence after the prize exchange
- Language: this text is a translation; the Japanese text is the original, and where the two differ the Japanese holds

## Preamble

The purpose of P-Lens (hereinafter "this system") is to record the economic events that come with play on pachinko machines and pachislot machines, on the principles of double-entry bookkeeping.

In an ordinary household account book, these events have until now been dealt with in a single line: "Pachinko -12,000 yen".

In the course of play, however, many economic events of differing nature occur: the putting in of money, the acquisition and using up of tokens, the winning of balls, the saving of balls, prize exchange and others.

These Standards lay down one treatment for recording these events reasonably and consistently.

These Standards do not aim to follow economic events beyond the prize exchange at a parlor.

Bookkeeping has a boundary.

These Standards make that boundary clear.

## Chapter 1 — Basic Principles

### Article 1 (Purpose of the accounting)

1. The purpose of these Standards is to record the expenses and revenue of play, and the acquisition of tokens and of prizes, by one consistent method.

2. The books shall be kept by double entry, and that debits equal credits shall be the principle.

3. That debits equal credits does not guarantee that the facts recorded are accurate.

4. Where a player has entered false information, this system may record that falsehood with extreme accuracy.

5. In the case of the preceding paragraph, hledger bears no responsibility.

### Article 2 (Honesty of the record)

1. A record shall be made on the basis of facts actually observed.

2. Estimates and observations shall be recorded apart, and a fact that is not known shall not be treated as known.

3. Where the balance in the book and the real balance do not agree, the book shall be adjusted by a reasonable method.

4. The real number of balls shall not be changed for the purpose of making the book right.

### Article 3 (Currencies particular to a parlor)

1. Tokens shall be managed as a unit of quantity of their own for each parlor and each rental rate.

2. 125 balls at parlor A and 125 balls at parlor B shall not be deemed the same asset merely because the quantities agree.

3. Where the distinction of the preceding paragraph is neglected, a player may hold balls enough in the book and yet be unable to put a single ball into the machine in front of them.

4. These Standards do not preclude treating the situation of the preceding paragraph as a marked lack of liquidity.

## Chapter 2 — Expenses and Revenue

### Article 4 (Expenses of play)

1. The economic burden of using up tokens shall be entered in the designated expense account.

2. An expense shall be recognised independently of the player's feelings.

3. Even where a player maintains that "today was a win, in effect", the expense shall not be reduced on the strength of that claim; provided, however, that this does not deny the satisfaction of mind in "it was fun, so it was a win".

### Article 5 (Revenue of play)

1. Economic value gained by play shall be entered in the designated revenue account.

2. Revenue shall be recognised on the basis of events that actually occurred.

3. The fact that the player in the next seat hit a jackpot shall not be entered as one's own revenue.

4. The preceding paragraph shall be applied with particular strictness.

### Article 6 (Treatment of cash)

1. Cash put into play shall be recorded.

2. The amount a player reports as having come back may be treated as an observation for totalling the results of play.

3. The preceding paragraph does not mean that this system books cash transactions after the prize exchange.

4. Knowing a figure and booking every transaction that led to that figure are separate matters.

### Article 7 (Cash runs one way)

1. This system books play at a parlor, up to the prize exchange.

2. Economic activity after prizes are acquired is outside what this system manages.

3. The cash account may fall as cash is put into play, but never rises by any entry this system makes.

4. This system has, in its standard model of transactions, no entry that turns special prizes into cash.

5. The two preceding paragraphs do not deny the existence of economic events that occur in the real world after prizes are acquired.

6. This system makes no judgement on events it has placed outside what it books.

7. On the fact that it makes no judgement under the preceding paragraph, it makes no further judgement either.

8. It is not that this system treats them as unknown. It does not book them.

9. The distinction of the preceding paragraph is of the utmost importance in these Standards.

## Chapter 3 — Prizes

### Article 8 (Accounting for prizes)

1. Where tokens are exchanged for prizes, the booking of those prizes ends with that exchange.

2. Prizes shall be divided into special prizes and general prizes, and each shall be entered in the terminal account laid down in the next Article and in Article 10.

3. The terminal accounts of the preceding paragraph are a classification of management accounting particular to this system, and do not alter the definition of equity in general financial accounting.

### Article 9 (Special prizes)

1. The terminal account for special prizes shall be as follows.

   `純資産:特殊景品` (equity: special prizes)

2. The account of the preceding paragraph is an account of management accounting particular to this system, to show that the booking of play has ended with the acquisition of special prizes, and does not alter the definition of equity in general financial accounting.

3. Special prizes shall not be treated as cash itself.

4. These Standards give no detailed definition of why special prizes are special.

5. It is acknowledged, at the least, that circumstances exist for which they should be managed apart from general prizes.

6. They are special.

### Article 10 (Odd prizes)

1. General prizes acquired with the quantity of tokens remaining short of a special prize shall be entered in the following terminal account.

   `純資産:端玉景品` (equity: odd prizes)

2. The prizes entered in the account of the preceding paragraph include sweets, drinks, food, daily goods and other articles, and the same applies to Yakult.

3. Even where the market price of a prize and its value at the exchange do not agree, the receipt of the prize shall not be denied for that reason alone.

4. That sweets received as a prize worth 40 balls are sold for less at a shop nearby is not a material accounting issue under these Standards; provided, however, that the player is not precluded from being dissatisfied.

### Article 11 (Prizes of higher grade)

1. The preceding Article applies also where a player acquires a general prize dearer than usual.

2. Whether a prize is sweets, household goods or some other article, the basic nature of the terminal account does not change.

3. These Standards set up no class system in accounting between Yakult and the like and fine confectionery.

4. All general prizes are, as a rule, terminated equally; provided, however, that this does not mean the prizes themselves are of equal value.

### Article 12 (Finality of the prize exchange)

1. This system does not follow the keeping, consumption, transfer or any other economic activity after prizes are acquired.

2. For special prizes too, transactions after their acquisition are outside what is booked.

3. The two preceding paragraphs do not deny the existence of economic events in the real world.

4. Ending the booking with the acquisition of prizes is a deliberate boundary in this system's accounting model.

## Chapter 4 — Translation and Adjustment

### Article 13 (Translation adjustment)

1. The part of a yen arising between the translated amount of tokens and the value of prizes, and other differences arising from the valuation and translation of tokens, may be adjusted, by the designated method, with the following account.

   `純資産:換算調整勘定` (equity: translation adjustment)

2. The account of the preceding paragraph is provided to keep the book consistent where several units of quantity are handled.

3. A difference arising from translation does not as a matter of course agree with the player's winning or losing.

4. Entering every difference in the account of paragraph 1 without making clear why the figures do not agree is not recommended.

5. The account of paragraph 1 is not a convenient box for hiding the book's inconsistencies unconditionally; provided, however, that it is acknowledged that the name of the account is extremely grand.

### Article 14 (Fractions)

1. Fractions shall be dealt with by a suitable method, according to the terms of each parlor and the needs of the bookkeeping.

2. Where a fraction arises, the necessary adjustment shall be made.

3. Whether a fraction may be disregarded shall be judged in the light of its nature and of the materiality of the amount.

4. That something is a fraction does not mean that it does not exist.

5. So long as odd balls exist, odd prizes too may exist.

6. So long as odd prizes exist, the possibility that Yakult becomes equity also exists.

7. This Article reinforces Article 10.

## Chapter 5 — Estimation

### Article 15 (Treatment of the theoretical value)

1. The theoretical value (期待値) is a theoretical figure calculated from a given probability model and given terms of calculation.

2. That the theoretical value is positive does not guarantee a profit in any one play.

3. Where observations are lacking, this system may make a probabilistic estimate.

4. A fact that was not observed does not become known merely because the number of calculations was increased.

## Chapter 6 — The Accounting Engine

### Article 16 (Respect for hledger)

1. This system uses hledger as the core of its accounting.

2. The accounting functions that hledger provides shall not be implemented again independently, except where there is reasonable cause.

3. The accounting policy particular to this system shall be managed apart from standard accounting theory and from hledger's functions.

4. This committee has not confirmed whether the developers of hledger foresaw the accounting for prizes laid down in these Standards.

5. For the fact that it has not been confirmed, deep respect is expressed.

### Article 17 (Knowledge asked of users)

1. Users are not required to have knowledge of double-entry bookkeeping, of Haskell or of any other programming language.

2. The aim is that even a user who does not know these Standards exist can keep their books without difficulty.

## Chapter 7 — Miscellaneous Provisions

### Article 18 (Priorities of these Standards)

1. In operating these Standards, weight shall be given to the following matters.

   1. Being based on actual records.
   2. Keeping the book consistent.
   3. Keeping estimates and facts apart.
   4. Not burdening users with needless complexity.
   5. Not following, to no purpose, economic activity after prizes are acquired.

2. The matters listed in the items of the preceding paragraph shall be construed so as to be consistent with one another.

### Article 19 (Scope of the booking, and responsibility)

1. This system is for recording the economic events of play, and does not guarantee a profit from play.

2. A real loss is not lessened because the booking was extremely accurate.

3. These Standards do not replace generally accepted accounting standards for businesses, nor the treatment of anything for tax.

4. Users shall use this system on the understanding that the treatment under these Standards differs from the classification of general financial accounting.

## Supplementary Provisions

### Paragraph 1 (Enactment and application)

These Standards are enacted as the document describing the accounting model applied in this system, and apply from 13 October 2026.

### Paragraph 2 (Relation to general accounting standards)

These Standards lay down the treatment of management accounting particular to this system, and do not replace Japan's accounting standards for businesses, the treatment of anything for tax, or any other public accounting standard. In particular, entering prizes in "equity" is a terminal treatment of this system's own, and does not deny the classification of prizes as assets in general financial accounting.

### Paragraph 3 (Amendment)

These Standards may be amended according to the circumstances of users, the specification of the software and the needs of the accounting; provided, however, that in an amendment full regard shall be had to consistency with books already recorded and to continuity with past accounting.

### Paragraph 4 (Management of prizes after the fact)

An amendment of these Standards does not require a retrospective stocktaking of prizes acquired in the past. The same applies to Yakult that has already been drunk.
